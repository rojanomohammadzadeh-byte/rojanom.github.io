Artist Statement

My work begins in the language of Persian miniature — its flattened space, its patient ornament, its gatherings of figures suspended mid-gesture — and asks what happens when that language is spoken today, by a hand that did not inherit it quietly.

I am drawn to the bazm: the courtly scene of music, wine, and company, once painted as a record of pleasure and belonging. In my paintings these gatherings persist, but the room has emptied. Figures repeat, fragment, or vanish from the frame entirely, leaving their color and posture behind like an afterimage.

Lapis, ochre, and the fine black line are not decoration here — they are memory carried in pigment. I use them to hold two times at once: the world the image came from, and the one it is looking at now.

What I want, finally, is not nostalgia. It is a gathering that keeps its seat open.

— Rojano Mohammadzadeh
