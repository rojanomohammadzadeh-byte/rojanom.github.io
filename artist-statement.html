Artist Statement

My work investigates forms of existence that institutions define as temporary but that increasingly become permanent ways of living. I am interested in the unstable thresholds produced by migration, where legality and illegality, arrival and departure, expiration and renewal, attachment and displacement overlap without ever fully resolving. Rather than understanding transition as a passage toward stability, I approach it as a condition that shapes the body, memory, and everyday life.

Working across drawing, installation, sculpture, and performance, I explore what it means to inhabit these overlapping states. My practice does not seek fixed identities or destinations, but lingers within suspension itself. The recurring figure throughout my work is the ghorbatī (غربتی)—someone living away from home, never completely here or there. In the United States this figure acquires another name: "alien." Rather than treating alienness as an identity, I understand it as an ongoing negotiation with legal systems, languages, histories, and expectations of belonging.
